## Abstract

We introduce a framework for representing term-rewriting systems as linear maps on vector spaces via *structurally linear encodings*. We first show that the existence question is trivial: every rewriting system admits a faithful linear representation (Theorem 3.1). The central contribution is a **commutation equation** (Theorem 4.1) that constrains the rule matrix \(A_R\) with respect to the algebraic context \(\mathcal M\) generated by the structural matrices. From this we derive an **impossibility theorem** (Theorem 5.3): over \(\mathbb F_2\), if \(\mathcal M\) acts irreducibly on \(V\) and the difference space satisfies \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min}=V\), then no faithful representation of a non-trivial rule exists. We obtain a **dimension lower bound** (Theorem 6.1), a **positive result for linear rules** (Theorem 7.2), and a **categorical extension to the linear \(\lambda\)-calculus** (Section 8), where the commutation equation becomes the naturality of evaluation and the dimension bound drops from exponential to polynomial. We state the classification of irreducible-context escapes as an open conjecture.

## 1. Introduction

Representing symbolic systems by linear-algebraic structures is a classical theme, ranging from regular representations of monoids to matrix interpretations for termination of term rewriting. The question we pose is different: **what conditions make a linear representation of a proof system faithful and non-trivial?**

We answer this question by isolating three interacting axes:

$$
\dim V \;\longleftrightarrow\; \text{faithfulness} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \text{algebraic richness of the context algebra.}
$$

Our framework distinguishes sharply between:

1. **Existence** — always possible (Section 3), and therefore mathematically vacuous.
2. **Faithful, low-dimensional, algebraically meaningful representation** — constrained by a commutation equation (Section 4), and impossible under irreducibility over \(\mathbb F_2\) (Section 5).
3. **The cost of escaping impossibility** — either a block decomposition, dimension growth, or loss of context-independence (Section 6).

We then extend the framework to the **linear \(\lambda\)-calculus** (Section 8), where structural linearity replaces positional linearity, and the dimension bound becomes polynomial.

### 1.1 Relation to Prior Work

- **Matrix interpretations** (Hofbauer–Lautemann 1989; Endrullis–Waldmann–Zantema 2008) use linear maps to prove *termination*. Our goal is complementary: we study *faithfulness* and *equality detection*.
- **Regular representation** of monoids guarantees a faithful representation but with unbounded dimension. We reproduce this as a triviality theorem (Theorem 3.1) and sharpen the question to the bounded case.
- **Categorical semantics of linear logic** (Girard 1987; Seely 1989) provides the natural setting for the \(\lambda\)-calculus extension. Our commutation equation is the vector-space shadow of the naturality of evaluation.
- **Context algebras** introduced here resemble operator algebras in representation theory, but are *derived* from the term structure rather than assumed.

## 2. Framework and Assumptions

### 2.1 Syntax

**Definition 2.1 (Signature and Terms).** Let \(\Sigma\) be a *ranked* alphabet (each symbol \(f \in \Sigma\) has an arity \(\mathrm{ar}(f) \ge 0\)). Let \(\mathcal T_\Sigma\) be the set of *ground terms* built from \(\Sigma\). Fix a **working slice** \(\mathcal W \subseteq \mathcal T_\Sigma\) closed under substitution into contexts.

**Definition 2.2 (Context).** A context \(C\) is a term with a single hole \(\Box\). We write \(C[t]\) for the result of filling the hole with \(t\). Let \(\mathcal C\) denote the set of contexts.

**Definition 2.3 (Rewriting System).** A rule \(R : \ell \to r\) where \(\ell, r \in \mathcal T_\Sigma(X)\) (terms with variables). Application in context \(C\) with substitution \(\sigma\) is written \(C[\ell\sigma] \to C[r\sigma]\).

### 2.2 Encoding Hypotheses

Fix a field \(K\), a vector space \(V\) over \(K\), and an encoding map \(\Phi : \mathcal T_\Sigma \to V\).

**(H1) Structural Linearity.** For each \(f \in \Sigma\) of arity \(n\), there exist matrices \(M_1^{(f)}, \dots, M_n^{(f)} \in \mathrm{End}(V)\) and a vector \(s_f \in V\) such that for all \(t_1, \dots, t_n \in \mathcal W\):

$$
\Phi(f(t_1, \dots, t_n)) = \sum_{i=1}^n M_i^{(f)}\,\Phi(t_i) + s_f.
$$

**(H2) Faithfulness.** \(\Phi|_{\mathcal W}\) is injective.

**(H3) Context Independence.** For each rule \(R\), there exists a unique matrix \(A_R \in \mathrm{End}(V)\) such that for all \(C \in \mathcal C\) and admissible \(\sigma\):

$$
\Phi(C[r\sigma]) = A_R\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]).
$$

**(H4) Closure.** If \(t \in \mathcal W\) and \(C \in \mathcal C\), then \(C[t] \in \mathcal W\).

**Definition 2.4 (Non-trivial Rule).** A rule \(R\) is *non-trivial with respect to* \(\Phi\) if there exists \(\sigma\) with \(\Phi(\ell\sigma) \neq \Phi(r\sigma)\).

**Definition 2.5 (Context Algebra).** The *context algebra* is the subalgebra
$$
\mathcal M := \langle M_i^{(f)} : f \in \Sigma,\ 1 \le i \le \mathrm{ar}(f) \rangle \subseteq \mathrm{End}(V)
$$
generated by the structural matrices.

## 3. Triviality of the Existence Question

**Theorem 3.1 (Regular Representation).** Let \(S\) be any countable set and \(R : S \to S\) any transformation system. There exist a vector space \(V\), a faithful encoding \(\Phi : S \to V\), and an assignment \(R \mapsto A_R\) satisfying

$$
\Phi(R(s)) = A_R\,\Phi(s), \qquad A_{R_2 \circ R_1} = A_{R_2} A_{R_1}.
$$

**Proof.** Let \(V = K^{(S)}\) with basis \(\{e_s\}_{s \in S}\), and set \(\Phi(s) = e_s\). Define \(A_R(e_s) = e_{R(s)}\) on the basis and extend linearly. Faithfulness and composition follow immediately. \(\square\)

**Remark 3.2.** Theorem 3.1 shows that the existence question is *mathematically vacuous*. The correct research question is: does a representation exist that is simultaneously (i) *low-dimensional*, (ii) *faithful*, and (iii) *algebraically rich*, i.e., satisfying the commutation equation of Section 4? This is what the rest of the paper investigates.

## 4. The Commutation Equation

We first fix the notation. For a binary symbol \(h\), write

$$
\Phi(h(t,c)) = M_h\,\Phi(t) + N_h\,\Phi(c) + s_h,
$$

where \(M_h := M_1^{(h)}\) and \(N_h := M_2^{(h)}\).

**Theorem 4.1 (Commutation Equation).** Under (H1)–(H4), for each binary symbol \(h\) and each position \(i \in \{1,2\}\), there exists a vector \(w_{h,i}(c)\) (depending on the symbol, the position, and the constant \(c\), but not on the context or the substitution) such that

$$
[M_i^{(h)},\, A_R]\,\Phi(\ell\sigma) = w_{h,i}(c) \qquad \forall \sigma,
$$

where \([\cdot, \cdot]\) denotes the Lie bracket \([X,Y] = XY - YX\). Consequently, for every pair \((\sigma, \sigma')\):

$$
[M_i^{(h)},\, A_R]\,\bigl(\Phi(\ell\sigma) - \Phi(\ell\sigma')\bigr) = 0. \tag{4.1}
$$

**Proof.** Take \(h\) binary and position \(i=1\) (the other case is identical). Let \(C_1 = h(\Box, c)\) be the context. By (H3):

$$
\Phi(h(C[r\sigma], c)) = A_R\,\Phi(h(C[\ell\sigma], c)).
$$

Expanding both sides by (H1):

$$
M_h\,\Phi(C[r\sigma]) + N_h\,\Phi(c) + s_h = A_R\bigl(M_h\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]) + N_h\,\Phi(c) + s_h\bigr).
$$

Using (H3) again for \(C\): \(\Phi(C[r\sigma]) = A_R\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma])\). Substituting:

$$
M_h A_R\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]) + N_h\,\Phi(c) + s_h = A_R M_h\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]) + A_R N_h\,\Phi(c) + A_R s_h.
$$

Rearranging:

$$
[M_h, A_R]\,\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]) = (A_R N_h - N_h)\,\Phi(c) + (A_R - I)\,s_h =: w_{h,1}(c).
$$

The right-hand side is independent of \(C\). Setting \(C = \Box\) yields the claim. Subtracting the equations for \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma'\) gives (4.1). \(\square\)

**Interpretation.** The rule matrix \(A_R\) must **commute** with each structural matrix \(M_i^{(h)}\), at least on the span of differences \(\Phi(\ell\sigma) - \Phi(\ell\sigma')\). This is the *algebraic heart* of the framework.

## 5. Impossibility Under Irreducibility

**Definition 5.1 (Difference Space).** For a rule \(R : \ell \to r\), define

$$
\mathcal D_\ell := \mathrm{span}\bigl\{\Phi(C[\ell\sigma]) - \Phi(C'[\ell\sigma']) \;:\; C, C' \in \mathcal C,\ \sigma, \sigma' \text{ admissible}\bigr\} \subseteq V,
$$

and the *reduced* difference space

$$
\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} := \mathrm{span}\{\Phi(\ell\sigma) - \Phi(\ell\sigma') : \sigma, \sigma' \text{ admissible}\}.
$$

**Lemma 5.2 (Non-vanishing).** Under (H2) and (H4), if \(\ell\) is non-constant (i.e., there exist \(\sigma, \sigma'\) with \(\ell\sigma \neq \ell\sigma'\) in \(\mathcal W\)), then \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} \neq 0\), and hence \(\mathcal D_\ell \neq 0\).

**Proof.** If \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} = 0\), then \(\Phi(\ell\sigma) = \Phi(\ell\sigma')\) for all \(\sigma, \sigma'\). Since \(\ell\sigma \neq \ell\sigma'\) as elements of \(\mathcal W\), this contradicts (H2). \(\square\)

**Theorem 5.3 (Impossibility over \(\mathbb F_2\)).** Work over \(K = \mathbb F_2\). Under (H1)–(H4), assume:
- \(\Phi\) is faithful (H2);
- \(R\) is non-trivial;
- \(\mathcal M\) acts irreducibly on \(V\);
- \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} = V\).

Then no representation exists: necessarily \(A_R \in \{0, I\}\), and each case contradicts the hypotheses.

**Proof.** By Theorem 4.1, every \(M_i^{(h)}\) satisfies \([M_i^{(h)}, A_R]|_{\mathcal D_\ell^{\min}} = 0\). Since \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} = V\), we have \([M_i^{(h)}, A_R] = 0\) for all \(h, i\). Hence \(A_R\) commutes with every generator of \(\mathcal M\), i.e.,

$$
A_R \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal M}(V).
$$

By Schur's lemma, if \(\mathcal M\) is irreducible, then \(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal M}(V)\) is a division algebra. Over \(K = \mathbb F_2\), the only division algebra is \(\mathbb F_2 \cdot I = \{0, I\}\). Thus \(A_R \in \{0, I\}\).

- **Case \(A_R = 0\):** Then \(\Phi(r\sigma) = 0\) for all \(\sigma\), so two distinct elements of \(\mathcal W\) collide in the image, contradicting (H2).
- **Case \(A_R = I\):** Then \(\Phi(r\sigma) = \Phi(\ell\sigma)\) for all \(\sigma\), contradicting either (H2) (if \(\ell\sigma \neq r\sigma\)) or non-triviality (if \(\ell\sigma = r\sigma\)).

Contradiction. \(\square\)

**Remark 5.4 (General Field).** Over a general field \(K\), the division algebra is \(K \cdot I\), so the conclusion becomes \(A_R = \lambda I\) with \(\lambda \in K\). If all \(s_f = 0\) (purely linear encoding), then \(\lambda \neq 0, 1\) may be possible. This is why we restrict Theorem 5.3 to \(K = \mathbb F_2\), which is the natural setting for proof-theoretic applications.

**Remark 5.5 (Condition \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} = V\)).** This condition is not automatic. It holds in natural cases: if the encoding is "free" (meaning \(\Phi(\mathcal W)\) spans \(V\)) and \(\ell\) has at least one variable ranging over enough values. In the general case, when \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} \neq V\), we obtain a weaker conclusion: \(A_R\) commutes with \(\mathcal M\) only on \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min}\).

**Corollary 5.6 (Classification of Escapes).** If impossibility fails (i.e., a faithful representation of a non-trivial \(R\) exists), then one of the following must hold:

1. \(\mathcal M\) is **reducible** (a non-trivial invariant subspace exists);
2. \(\dim V\) **grows** (the condition \(\mathcal D_\ell^{\min} = V\) fails);
3. **Context independence (H3) is violated** (a position-dependent family \(\{A_R^{(p)}\}_p\) replaces the uniform \(A_R\)).

This is a *classification of escapes* rather than a theorem in the strict sense; cases (2) and (3) are studied in Section 6.

## 6. Dimension Lower Bound

**Proposition 6.1 (Dimension Bound for Equality Detection).** Let \(R\) be a rule with repeated left-hand side, \(\ell = f(x,x)\). Let \(\mathcal W_k\) be the slice of ground terms of size \(\le k\). Under (H2), to distinguish \(\Phi(a) = \Phi(b)\) from \(\Phi(a) \neq \Phi(b)\) on \(\mathcal W_k\), we require

$$
\dim V \ge \log_2 |\mathcal W_k|.
$$

**Proof.** Over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(|\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi| \le 2^{\dim V}\). Faithfulness on \(\mathcal W_k\) requires \(2^{\dim V} \ge |\mathcal W_k|\). \(\square\)

**Corollary 6.2 (Growth Rate).** If \(\Sigma\) contains at least one symbol of arity \(\ge 2\), then \(|\mathcal W_k|\) grows **exponentially** in \(k\), hence

$$
\dim V \ge c \cdot k \quad \text{for some constant } c > 0.
$$

If \(\Sigma\) contains only unary symbols and constants, \(|\mathcal W_k|\) grows polynomially, and the bound is weaker.

**Remark 6.3.** This is the *quantitative price of faithfulness*: a faithful representation capable of detecting equality forces dimension growth linear in the size of the working slice. This gives a precise meaning to "no free lunch."

## 7. Positive Results: Linear Rules

**Definition 7.1 (Linear Rule).** A rule \(R : \ell \to r\) is *linear* if every variable appears **exactly once** in \(\ell\), and every variable in \(r\) appears in \(\ell\).

**Theorem 7.2 (Representation of Linear Rules).** Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite signature and \(R\) a linear rule. There exist a vector space \(V\) of dimension \(O(|\Sigma|)\) and a faithful encoding \(\Phi\) satisfying (H1)–(H4) that represents \(R\) by a block-diagonal matrix.

**Proof (Construction).** Let \(V = \bigoplus_{f \in \Sigma} \bigoplus_{i=1}^{\mathrm{ar}(f)} K^{d_f}\) with \(d_f = \mathrm{ar}(f)\). For each symbol \(f\), define \(M_i^{(f)}\) as the inclusion into the component \((f, i)\), and set \(s_f = 0\). For each \(t\), \(\Phi(t)\) carries symbol and position information. A linear rule translates to a simple permutation of components, which is linear and faithful. \(\square\)

**Theorem 7.3 (Compositional Closure).** The representation constructed in Theorem 7.2 satisfies \(A_{R_2 \circ R_1} = A_{R_2} A_{R_1}\), since each \(A_R\) is a block permutation matrix.

**Remark 7.4.** Theorem 7.2 **does not** extend to non-linear rules. A counterexample: the rule \(f(x,x) \to g(x)\) requires equality detection, which by Proposition 6.1 forces growing dimension. Hence **linearity is the boundary** between representability and impossibility.

## 8. Extension: Linear \(\lambda\)-Calculus

### 8.1 Why the Original Framework Needs Adaptation

The original framework (H1) assumes **positional compositionality**: each symbol \(f\) of arity \(n\) is translated by fixed matrices \(M_1^{(f)}, \dots, M_n^{(f)}\) acting on **positions** of subterms. This works well for first-order term rewriting, where syntax is a free algebra.

The \(\lambda\)-calculus introduces two features that break this framework:

1. **Binding.** In \(\lambda x.M\), the variable \(x\) is bound inside \(M\). The translation \(\Phi(\lambda x.M)\) cannot depend on \(\Phi(M)\) alone, because \(M\) may contain \(x\) as a free variable, whereas \(\lambda x.M\) does not.
2. **Non-positional composition.** \(MN\) is not merely "placing \(N\) in a slot of \(M\)"; the slot is determined dynamically.

**Proposition 8.1 (Failure of H1 in \(\lambda\)-Calculus).** There is no faithful encoding \(\Phi : \Lambda \to V\) satisfying (H1) for the abstraction and application constructors of the untyped \(\lambda\)-calculus.

**Proof.** Suppose there exists an abstraction matrix \(A_\lambda\) such that \(\Phi(\lambda x.M) = A_\lambda \Phi(M) + s_\lambda\). From the \(\alpha\)-equivalences \(\lambda x.x = \lambda y.y\) and \(\lambda x.y = \lambda y.x\), we obtain

$$
A_\lambda(\Phi(x) - \Phi(y)) = 0.
$$

If \(\Phi(x) \neq \Phi(y)\) (by faithfulness), then \(A_\lambda\) is not injective, and abstraction loses information. If \(\Phi(x) = \Phi(y)\), faithfulness fails. Contradiction. \(\square\)

**Lesson.** Positional compositionality must be replaced by **categorical compositionality**, where binding is handled by the structure of the ambient category rather than by fixed matrices.

### 8.2 Simple Linear \(\lambda\)-Calculus

We restrict to the **linear** fragment of the \(\lambda\)-calculus (in the sense of linear logic): each variable is used **exactly once**.

**Types.** \(A ::= \alpha \mid A \multimap B \mid A \otimes B \mid I\)

**Contexts.** \(\Gamma = (x_1 : A_1, \dots, x_n : A_n)\)

**Terms** (typing rules):
- Variable: \(x : A\) in context \((x : A)\)
- Abstraction: \(\lambda x.M : A \multimap B\) if \(M : B\) in \(\Gamma, x : A\)
- Application: \(MN : B\) if \(M : A \multimap B\) and \(N : A\)
- Tensor: \(\langle M, N \rangle : A \otimes B\) if \(M : A\), \(N : B\)
- Destructuring: \(\mathsf{let}\,\langle x, y \rangle = M\,\mathsf{in}\,N : C\)
- Unit: \(* : I\)

**Reductions.**

$$
(\beta)\quad (\lambda x.M)N \to M[x := N]
$$

$$
(\eta)\quad \lambda x.Mx \to M \quad (x \notin FV(M))
$$

$$
(\otimes\beta)\quad \mathsf{let}\,\langle x, y \rangle = \langle M, N \rangle\,\mathsf{in}\,P \to P[x := M, y := N]
$$

### 8.3 Categorical Semantics

**Definition 8.2 (Linear Representation, Adapted Version).** A linear representation of linear \(\lambda\)-calculus consists of:

1. A symmetric monoidal closed category \((\mathbf{Vec}_K, \otimes, \multimap, K)\).
2. An interpretation \(\llbracket-\rrbracket : \mathbf{Types} \to \mathbf{Vec}_K\) such that

$$
\llbracket A \multimap B \rrbracket = \mathrm{Hom}(\llbracket A \rrbracket, \llbracket B \rrbracket),\quad
\llbracket A \otimes B \rrbracket = \llbracket A \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket B \rrbracket,\quad
\llbracket I \rrbracket = K.
$$

3. For each term \(M\) in context \(\Gamma\), a linear map

$$
\llbracket M \rrbracket_\Gamma : \llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket \to \llbracket A \rrbracket,
$$

defined by structural induction.

**Adapted Structural Compositionality (H1').** Instead of positional linearity, we require **functoriality**:

$$
\llbracket x \rrbracket = \mathrm{id},\quad
\llbracket \lambda x.M \rrbracket = \mathrm{curry}(\llbracket M \rrbracket),\quad
\llbracket MN \rrbracket = \mathrm{ev}(\llbracket M \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket N \rrbracket).
$$

**Faithfulness (H2').** \(\llbracket-\rrbracket\) is injective on \(\beta\eta\)-normal forms.

**Context Independence (H3').** Reduction rules correspond to natural transformations; specifically, \(\beta\) is the natural transformation \(\mathrm{ev}_{A,B} : (A \multimap B) \otimes A \to B\).

**Closure (H4').** The translation is compatible with substitution.

### 8.4 Concrete Representation in Vector Spaces

Fix a field \(K\) and a base dimension \(d \ge 2\). Define:

$$
\llbracket \alpha \rrbracket = K^d,\quad
\llbracket A \multimap B \rrbracket = \mathrm{Hom}(\llbracket A \rrbracket, \llbracket B \rrbracket),\quad
\llbracket A \otimes B \rrbracket = \llbracket A \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket B \rrbracket.
$$

**Lemma 8.3.** For a type \(A\) of size \(n\) and arrow depth \(\delta\):

$$
\dim \llbracket A \rrbracket = d^{\,p(n, \delta)}
$$

where \(p\) is a polynomial.

**Example 8.4.** With \(d = 2\):
- \(\dim \llbracket \alpha \rrbracket = 2\)
- \(\dim \llbracket \alpha \multimap \alpha \rrbracket = 4\)
- \(\dim \llbracket (\alpha \multimap \alpha) \multimap \alpha \rrbracket = 8\)
- \(\dim \llbracket ((\alpha \multimap \alpha) \multimap \alpha) \multimap \alpha \rrbracket = 16\)

Growth is exponential in **type depth**, but constant in term size — the key difference from the original framework.

### 8.5 \(\beta\) and \(\eta\) as Natural Transformations

The central operations of \(\lambda\)-calculus are:
- **Substitution** \(M[x := N]\) — a metalevel operation
- **\(\beta\)-reduction** \((\lambda x.M)N \to M[x := N]\) — a rule

In categorical semantics:

$$
\llbracket M[x := N] \rrbracket = \llbracket M \rrbracket \circ (\mathrm{id}_\Gamma \otimes \llbracket N \rrbracket)
$$

$$
\llbracket (\lambda x.M)N \rrbracket = \mathrm{ev}(\llbracket \lambda x.M \rrbracket \otimes \llbracket N \rrbracket) = \llbracket \lambda x.M \rrbracket(\llbracket N \rrbracket)
$$

**Theorem 8.5 (\(\beta\)-Commutation).** The following diagram commutes:

$$
\llbracket (\lambda x.M)N \rrbracket = \llbracket M \rrbracket \circ (\mathrm{id}_\Gamma \otimes \llbracket N \rrbracket) = \llbracket M[x := N] \rrbracket.
$$

**Interpretation.** This is the precise counterpart of the commutation equation \([M_h, A_R] = 0\) from Section 4:

- **There:** the rule matrix **commutes** with context matrices.
- **Here:** \(\beta\) is not a matrix \(A_R \in \mathrm{End}(V)\), but a **natural transformation** \(\mathrm{ev} : \mathrm{Hom}(A, B) \otimes A \to B\).
- "Commutation" here is the **naturality** of \(\mathrm{ev}\), a shadow of the symmetric monoidal closed structure.

**Theorem 8.6 (Naturality of Application).** For every \(f : A' \to A\) and \(g : B \to B'\), the following diagram commutes:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\mathrm{Hom}(A, B) \otimes A' & \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev} \circ (\mathrm{Hom}(f, B) \otimes \mathrm{id})} & B \\
\downarrow \scriptstyle{\mathrm{Hom}(A, g) \otimes f} & & \downarrow \scriptstyle{g} \\
\mathrm{Hom}(A', B') \otimes A' & \xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}} & B'
\end{array}
$$

This is the **commutation equation** in categorical form.

### 8.6 Faithfulness and Polynomial Dimension Bound

**Theorem 8.7 (Soundness and Completeness).** For linear \(\lambda\)-calculus:
1. **(Soundness)** If \(M =_{\beta\eta} N\), then \(\llbracket M \rrbracket = \llbracket N \rrbracket\).
2. **(Completeness)** If \(\llbracket M \rrbracket = \llbracket N \rrbracket\) in all interpretations, then \(M =_{\beta\eta} N\).

**Proof sketch.** Soundness follows from the categorical axioms. Completeness follows from the construction of the **free symmetric monoidal closed category** on the base types: the syntactic category of linear \(\lambda\)-calculus is free, and the interpretation is the universal functor. \(\square\)

**Theorem 8.8 (Faithfulness in \(\mathbf{Vec}_K\)).** For simple linear \(\lambda\)-calculus, the interpretation in \(\mathbf{Vec}_K\) (with base dimension \(d \ge 2\), and field \(K\) infinite or of characteristic 0) is faithful on \(\beta\eta\)-normal forms.

**Theorem 8.9 (Polynomial Dimension Bound).** For linear \(\lambda\)-calculus, the number of \(\beta\eta\)-normal terms of type \(A\) in a fixed context \(\Gamma\) is **finite**, bounded by a polynomial in the size of \(A\) and \(\Gamma\).

**Proof sketch.** By induction on the structure of types and contexts. Each base type contributes at most one variable use; each arrow type introduces at most one application; no duplication occurs. The linearity constraint prevents exponential blow-up. \(\square\)

**Corollary 8.10.** The dimension required for a faithful representation is **polynomial** in the size of the type, in contrast with the exponential bound of Section 6.

**Remark 8.11.** This is the precise meaning of "linearity buys a free lunch": the exponential blow-up of the general case is tamed to polynomial by forbidding duplication.

### 8.7 Comparison with the Original Framework

| Aspect | Original Framework (Sections 4–6) | \(\lambda\)-Extension (Section 8) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Free algebra (positional) | Categorical semantics |
| Compositionality | (H1) positional | (H1') functorial |
| Representation | Matrices \(A_R \in \mathrm{End}(V)\) | Natural transformations |
| Commutation equation | \([M_h, A_R] = 0\) | Naturality of \(\mathrm{ev}\) |
| Dimension bound | \(\Omega(k \log|\Sigma|)\) exponential | Polynomial in type size |
| Faithfulness | On the working slice | On \(\beta\eta\)-normal forms |
| Rigidity | Impossibility under irreducibility | No impossibility (space suffices) |

### 8.8 What Breaks When Duplication Is Added?

The linearity constraint is essential. When duplication is allowed (general \(\lambda\)-calculus, or linear logic with "!"), the picture changes:

**Proposition 8.12.** Adding contraction (duplication) to linear \(\lambda\)-calculus yields:
1. The number of normal forms grows **super-polynomially** (exponentially in typical cases).
2. The dimension bound becomes **exponential**, as in Section 6.
3. The commutation equation acquires a **multiplicative** form (multiple \(M_h\)'s appear).

**Connection to the main theory.** The original framework (Sections 2–6) is the "duplication-aware" case, where the rewriting system encodes the necessary bookkeeping (in the form of repeated variables). Linear \(\lambda\)-calculus is the "duplication-free" special case where the framework simplifies dramatically.

**Conjecture 8.13.** General \(\lambda\)-calculus (with duplication) admits a faithful linear representation if and only if one of the following holds:
- The type/context is restricted to exclude "unbounded duplication"; or
- The space \(V\) is allowed to grow (Hilbert-space case, Open Problem 1); or
- The representation is allowed to be "non-uniform" (position-dependent).

**Conclusion.** Linearity is the **precise boundary** between polynomial and exponential complexity in the linear representation of proofs. This gives a sharp meaning to the classification in the theory of bounds (Section 6): the obstacle is not "equality" per se, but **variable duplication**, which forces equality detection on spaces of growing dimension.

### 8.9 Comprehensive Example: Linear Church Numeral

Let \(\alpha\) be a base type and \(V_\alpha = K^2\). Consider the type:

$$
A = (\alpha \multimap \alpha) \multimap (\alpha \multimap \alpha).
$$

**The unique closed term** (up to \(\beta\eta\)) of this type is:

$$
M = \lambda f.\lambda x.fx.
$$

**Interpretation.**
- \(\llbracket \alpha \multimap \alpha \rrbracket = \mathrm{Hom}(K^2, K^2) = M_2(K)\)
- \(\llbracket A \rrbracket = \mathrm{Hom}(M_2(K), M_2(K))\)
- \(\llbracket M \rrbracket = \mathrm{id}_{M_2(K)}\)

**Faithfulness.** No other closed term of this type exists in linear \(\lambda\)-calculus (since \(f\) cannot be used twice). Hence the interpretation is automatically faithful, with required dimension \(4 \times 4 = 16\).

**Comparison with general \(\lambda\)-calculus.** There, closed terms of type \(A\) include \(\lambda f.\lambda x.f(fx)\) (Church numeral 2), \(\lambda f.\lambda x.f(f(fx))\) (Church numeral 3), ..., forcing \(\dim V_\alpha = \infty\) for a faithful representation of all terms. This is the essential difference between the two cases.

## 9. Open Problems

1. **Hilbert-space case.** Let \(V\) be a Hilbert space and the elements of \(\mathcal M\) be bounded operators. Does a middle ground exist combining full faithfulness, representability, and non-trivial representational richness (invariants, decompositions, commutation theory)? This is the possible path to a "representation theory of proofs" in the deep sense.

2. **Proof of Conjecture (block structure).** Under what additional conditions on \(\mathcal M\) or \(\Phi\) can the three-fold decomposition of Corollary 5.6 be proven? The current evidence is partial:
   - The "modulo-2 counting" example (Endrullis et al.) shows that the equality block \(E\) is necessary.
   - Simple \(\lambda\)-calculus examples show that the context block \(V_{\mathrm{ctx}}\) separates naturally.
   - But **proving the decomposition is forced** remains open.

3. **General field.** When can \(A_R = \lambda I\) with \(\lambda \notin \{0, 1\}\) occur? What is its semantic interpretation?

4. **Non-linear compositionality (breaking H1).** Can non-linear (feature-gated) maps absorb some impossibility cases while preserving compositionality?

5. **Higher ranks and quantifiers.** The precise classification of \(\forall\)-obstructions and higher-rank operators within this framework is open.

6. **Linear logic with exponentials.** Extend the \(\lambda\)-calculus analysis to full linear logic with "!" (bang), where duplication re-enters in a controlled way. Is the dimension bound still polynomial in some restricted fragment?

---

## 10. Conclusion

We have presented a rigorous framework for linear representations of proof transformations, with the following contributions:

- **Triviality theorem** (Theorem 3.1): existence is vacuous.
- **Commutation equation** (Theorem 4.1): the algebraic heart of the theory.
- **Impossibility theorem** (Theorem 5.3): over \(\mathbb F_2\), irreducibility blocks non-trivial faithful representation.
- **Dimension bound** (Proposition 6.1): exponential in the working slice size for equality detection.
- **Positive result** (Theorem 7.2): linear rules are always representable.
- **Categorical extension** (Section 8): linear \(\lambda\)-calculus yields polynomial dimension bounds and naturality as the commutation equation.

The framework isolates **variable duplication** as the precise source of all obstructions. This gives a mathematically rigorous meaning to the intuition that "equality detection is expensive," and identifies the boundary between polynomial and exponential complexity in linear proof representation.
